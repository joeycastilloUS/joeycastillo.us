Runbooks — a plural config surface in the Argus Zendesk app

Port Manage the Triage into the console as a library, not a page.

Paired per Rule 1 — Opus driver, Fable navigator, concurrent then reconciled. Navigator owns the verdict; both divergences are surfaced below, none resolved silently. Target: demo tomorrow, operator logs in.

1 · The need

The triage config exists and is real, but it lives in a dev tool that nobody logs into. It needs to be in Argus, where the work already happens. Three constraints shape everything:

  • The demo is tomorrow and Joey logs in — not the manager. Manager identity and Zendesk login are explicitly post-demo. That removes the hardest blocker from tomorrow's path and we should not spend a single hour on it.
  • This is one of many. The repo already holds three live cards plus a retired one. A single-purpose page would be rework inside a week.
  • Nothing may lie. The applier does not exist. The surface has to say so in words, not hide it.

2 · Raw user stories

As the operator, I open Argus, land in the Zendesk app, and see every automated procedure I own in one list — not just the triage one.

As the operator, I open one, see what it does in demands I can read, see which settings are mine, which are IT's, and which are locked — and see the options each one actually permits.

As the operator demoing this, I can point at the screen and say "and this is where the support manager will tune it" without anything on screen being a mockup.

As the operator, when I change a value I am told exactly what did and did not happen.

4 · Literal naming — where we disagree with you

“Plan” is already taken, by the file sitting next to it

You floated plan as the external word for a .pi and asked if we like it. We do not, and the reason is concrete rather than aesthetic — the .si is already called the plan, in our own files:

mendo-nps-detractor-triage.pi:5   "The cortex = this .pi + its .si (the plan) + the .sm"
mendo-nps-detractor-triage.si:2   "SKILL INTENT (the execution plan)"
mendo-nps-detractor-triage.si:17  plan:          ← the literal parse token

Adopting it externally means you say “plan” meaning .pi while every file, every parser, and metal's own ~p command say “plan” meaning .si. Also taken: be/plans/, and the console already uses Plan as a dry-run verb (:3296 “Plan clone (dry)”). And “Plans” would sit in the tab rail directly beside “Programs.”

The answer is two words, not one: Runbook outside, playbook inside

The operator's surface says Runbook — the word an operations team already owns. They are not managing an engine concept; they are managing the procedure their shop runs on.

The runtime keeps playbook, and it was right all along:

src/sdk/nous/action/skill/pirun/pirun.h:5
  "A .pi playbook is POLICY AS DATA: frontmatter + a protocol[] of structured units"

That is the engine describing what it holds — policy, as data, loaded and sealed. No operator ever reads it. The two words were never required to match, and forcing them to would make one of them lie. Internal names the artifact's nature; external names its job.

“Plan” is rejected for both. It already means the companion .si — in the .pi's own header, in the .si's own header, and as the literal plan: parse token.

Consequence: the external rename touches documents and labels only. Zero C99 changes — which is also why it is safe during a freeze: nothing that executes is modified.

5.3 — Why the missing applier is a feature on stage

Do not apologise for it in the demo. It is the safety property: biRT means pi_run “refuses to load on drift” (.pi:8-9). Config cannot silently change under a sealed card — stage, then the owner applies, then it reseals. That is the designed path. The screen says so in plain words, which is exactly what a support manager should be told before they are handed the stick.

Where the pair diverged

Divergence 1 — where staged edits go. The navigator's primary pick was the existing /settings round-trip (one key in settingsKeys(), syncs across machines free). The driver took the navigator's own caution as decisive: that Firestore doc is server-side gate authority (Program.cs:673-682). Writing runbook config into the document that decides who may do what — the night before a demo — is the wrong risk. Resolved: localStorage for the demo, its own document post-demo. Navigator concurred on review.

Divergence 2 — the name. Driver initially accepted “plan” as the operator's call. Navigator refused it with file:line evidence. The operator then settled it: Runbook on the outside, playbook in the runtime — the internal name was already correct. Surfaced to the operator rather than resolved — the name is Joey's to pick.

Unknowns

  • Whether acmedevUS/zendesk-bridge can serve a live runbook list — out of repo, unverified. Bake is chosen partly because it does not depend on the answer.
  • How many runbooks this scales to before the flat library needs grouping. Four is fine; forty is not.
  • Whether the manager should ever see IT and Locked rows at all, or only Ops. Deferred with the login.

When to revisit

The moment any of these fires: the applier lands (staging moves off localStorage); a second person needs to log in (the whole auth question reopens and this design does not answer it); a .pi is edited and the baked snapshot goes stale in front of someone — that is the signal the live endpoint is no longer optional.
