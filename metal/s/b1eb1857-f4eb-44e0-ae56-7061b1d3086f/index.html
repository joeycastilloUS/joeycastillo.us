The guest is not the envelope

Five visible defects on one screen, from two causes. The ticket's identity is built from whoever sent the mail, and a second ticket is created that inherits none of the right settings.

Paired — driver writes, navigator reviews with authority to block. Design only; nothing is built and no code is touched.

And the footer is the smoking gun: "This comment was submitted by Guest Feedback on behalf of the author." Zendesk is stating that the comment's author is the guest — and Zendesk never emails anyone about their own comment. That sentence is the missing email, printed on screen.

A coupon was spent for it. A code was claimed and gone — burned on a message nobody received.

Correction the navigator forced, and it inverts the risk: the first draft said "every retry burns another." False. The gate tag-claims the ticket before the pipeline runs, and the code claim is idempotent per ticket — a re-sweep returns the code that ticket already owns and cannot reach the send. The live code appears in exactly one ticket.

The real gap is the opposite one: the ticket is marked handled before the send, so a send that fails is never retried. Not a double spend — a silently lost message with a coupon already spent against it. That is what happened here, four times over.

Two causes, not one — and the split matters

Three defects come from the identity. Two come from creating a second ticket at all.

The first draft of this document claimed all five shared one cause. That was wrong, and the correction strengthens the recommendation: the alert tickets already carry the correct Guest Relations brand and form. Replying on the alert ticket inherits both for free — two of the five defects disappear simply by stopping the second ticket, with no field-setting at all.

On identity: JotForm mails the submission to a support address, and Zendesk does what email systems do — it makes the sender the requester. The guest's real name and address sit in the body, adjacent to fields Argus already parses, and nothing reads them.

Also worth knowing: the suppression on that ticket was doubly determined. The comment was authored by the requester and no trigger could have matched anyway (no group, wrong form). Fixing the author alone would not have delivered the mail.

Two ways, and they converge

A — Correct the identity after intake

Parse Guest Name and Guest Email from the body, create-or-update the Zendesk end-user with the correct name, then set the alert ticket's requester_id to that user. Reply on the alert ticket as an agent.

Keeps today's plumbing. No JotForm change, no new lane. It is the workaround that works this week.

Verified feasible: requester_id is mutable by PUT on an email-channel ticket; agents and admins can be requesters and do receive requester-notify mail; and a requester change with no public comment matches none of this account's notify triggers, so it fires nothing.

The guard that must be written down: only set the name when the matched user is an end-user. Skip or hold on an agent or admin. Without it, a create-or-update against the parsed address on this very ticket would rename the shared admin mailbox to "Mike M" — a live account belonging to staff, renamed by an automated recovery.

B — Stop carrying identity in an envelope

JotForm posts to the existing alert-create lane instead of sending mail, with the guest stated explicitly as fields. Zendesk never has to guess, because nothing is inferred from a sender address.

Stated precisely, because the first draft overpromised: the lane exists and is real, but today it accepts only {nps, overall, store, order_id, guest_email, feedback} — no guest name and no brand — and it deliberately creates with the neutral NPS-system requester. In other words the lane as built embodies D13, the very demand this path has to amend. So B is a JotForm webhook plus a bridge change to accept a guest name and set the requester and brand. The bridge is C#, and its source is not in these repos.

The argument that decides it: option A is correct only as long as JotForm keeps sending as the submitter. If it is ever configured to send from a fixed notification mailbox — or a real guest's mail fails SPF and gets rewritten — every guest collapses into one Zendesk user and every reply goes to that mailbox. That is not hypothetical: #737's requester is already a shared test mailbox. Option B is immune to however JotForm is configured, forever.

They converge, which is why this is not really a fork. Both make the form body authoritative for identity. A applies that rule after the fact; B applies it at the source. Do A now if you need it this week, and know that its logic is B's logic — the work is not thrown away.

What I would not do

  • Do not rescue the separate follow-up ticket by fixing its author. It lands on the wrong brand and the wrong form, splits the conversation into two tickets per guest, and leaves the guest's own ticket sitting unanswered. The second ticket should stop existing.
  • Do not trust the envelope even after fixing it once. That is the whole lesson of #737.
  • Do not rely on Zendesk's intelligent triage. It called a 2/10 detractor "Positive service feedback" on this very ticket. It is decoration here, not signal.

Open for you

  • A or B — workaround now, or go straight to the durable shape.
  • D13 contradicts the new reality. It specifies a neutral NPS-system requester for privacy and loop-prevention. On this path the requester must become the guest. Amend it, or someone will restore the neutral requester and silently break the reply path.
  • The subject the guest sees is the trigger's, not ours — "Follow Up On Your Mendo Feedback" never reaches them without a dedicated trigger. D9 owns that.
  • Whether the guest should be CC'd on their own alert at all — JotForm currently addresses them as well, so they receive the raw internal template from themselves.

Unknowns

  • Whether the bridge's recovery lane dedupes on its idempotency key — the C# is not in these repos, and the live coupon claim demonstrably happens there rather than in anything inspected here.
  • Whether the JotForm form can post a webhook at all. The product supports it; it is unverified for this form, and there are no JotForm credentials in these repos.
  • Whether creating a fresh end-user fires a welcome or verification mail under this account's settings — option A would create users routinely.
  • Where the live coupon vault actually lives and how a spent code is marked; the local store holds a different code series entirely.

When to revisit

Immediately if JotForm's notification sender changes — option A is only correct while it sends as the submitter, and the collapse it guards against is silent. Again when the bridge source becomes available, because two of the four unknowns above resolve the moment it can be read. And before prod arming regardless, since the send-failure gap (marked handled, never retried) loses guests one at a time with no alarm.
