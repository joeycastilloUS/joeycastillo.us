Two things fall straight out of that table, and one of them is a problem nobody has raised yet.

Good news: the gate is safe at the source

Email is required on the form. Every submission carries one. So "we found a guest email" will always be satisfiable for form-originated alerts — and Store and Order ID are required and typed, which is better than the legacy path where an upstream object-id was mis-mapped into both.

The problem: feedback is optional, and the whole triage assumes it exists

A guest can score us 0 out of 10 and submit with an empty comment box. The card has no answer for that:

DemandWhat it assumes
D3"Read the guest's feedback in their own words" — there are none
D4 / D5The illness panel judges the feedback verbatim. Two models are asked to rule on an empty string, and any error or silence counts as illness — so an empty comment plausibly escalates to a human every time
D10The empathy line "mirrors what the guest complained about." Nothing to mirror → the safe generic line, which is the correct fallback and the one part that already works

This is a design question for you, not a coding one. A 0-score with no comment is a real detractor who deserves recovery, but there is nothing to triage for illness. Options: treat empty feedback as safe-by-definition and go straight to the generic recovery; or treat it as not-actionable and route to a human; or make the field required on the form. The third is one click for Brendan and removes the question entirely — but it also costs you submissions, because required fields depress completion.

The decision: control the delivery

Make the form's submission arrive the way the default brand's alerts already arrive.

The markdown, the next-line Store value, the renamed field — none of that is inherent to the form. It is what happens when a structured submission is flattened into an email and Zendesk renders the HTML. The fix is not to teach the parser to read the flattening; it is to stop flattening.

ApproachVerdict
1Reformat the notification email to plain text matching the existing literalsWorks today, zero deploy. Brittle — one theme change in JotForm and it silently breaks again, exactly as it did this week
2Teach scan_alert to read mangled emailTouches the gate every brand's traffic passes through, to parse a format we do not have to accept in the first place
3JotForm submission → the existing alert-create lane, with a brandRecommended. The body is composed by our own bridge, so it matches the parser by construction. pi_run.c is untouched.

Why 3 is genuinely better and not just tidier: the alert body stops being a document that has to survive a mail client. It becomes a typed payload with required fields, from a form that already enforces them. The parser and the writer stay two halves of one contract we own — which is the arrangement that has been working for the default brand all along.

Where the two passes disagreed

Divergence 1 — the send guard. The 09:30 design proposed suppressing recovery sends for non-default brands until brand-carry lands. The navigator argued that under "changes are allowed" the guard is dominated: it is throwaway work and it blocks the exact thing Mendo HD wants to test. Driver concedes. Do the brand-carry (change 3) instead — it is smaller than building the guard and later removing it.

Divergence 2 — whether a real upstream exists. The navigator concluded the GR format was a pure testing artifact and that building for it would be solving the wrong problem. The driver had the same instinct. Both were wrong, and the operator supplied the correction. Recording it because the lesson generalises: two independent passes agreeing is not the same as being right, and neither of us thought to ask where the tickets came from.

Divergence 3 — validating against production. The navigator raised something the driver missed entirely and it survives this redesign: the parser has never been tested against a real production alert body. The documented legacy prod template uses column-aligned labels that the current extractor would mis-read, and may carry no feedback field at all. That is a separate, larger issue than this one — and it is the actual gate before arming prod, not this brand work.

Unknowns — the first one blocks the build

  • How does a JotForm submission become a Zendesk ticket today? Notification email, webhook, or a person forwarding it? The four tickets arrived from three different staff mailboxes, which suggests a person in the loop. Change 2 cannot be specified until this is known, and it is a question for Brendan, not an investigation.
  • Whether the empty-feedback case should escalate, recover generically, or be made impossible on the form. Operator decision.
  • Whether this form replaces the legacy upstream or runs alongside it. If alongside, there are two templates in play and the parser question returns — just not as an emergency.
  • Whether the seven stores are the real list or a test subset.

When to revisit

When the delivery mechanism is answered — that decides change 2 entirely. Before arming prod, regardless of this work, because of divergence 3. And if a second upstream template is ever confirmed to exist in production, at which point tolerant parsing stops being premature and becomes necessary.
