The device on BOH port 23

Store 040 — Mountain View · BOH switch Gi1/0/23 · client named EKAN

Short answer: the network side is healthy. Nothing is misconfigured and nothing is broken.

The device is on the correct VLAN, holding a valid address your MX handed it, and it has been steadily connected for at least 31 days without a single dropout.

The open question is not networking — it is identity. We cannot confirm from the network that this is an iPad, and we cannot tell you who owns it. It sits on your staff VLAN, on a port with no 802.1X, and we could not match it to any other device across your 117 stores.

One note on the screen you were looking at: this port is in the 040 — Mountain View network. “Mendocino Store” is the name of the staff Wi-Fi SSID broadcast at every location, which is why the page reads that way.

Most likelyWhat we think it is

The two explanations that fit all of the evidence, in order:

  • A stationary, always-on tablet — plausibly the iPad you can see — wired through a USB-to-Ethernet adapter or dock. This would explain the non-Apple MAC: a wired iPad always presents its adapter’s address, never Apple’s. It would also explain the zero PoE draw, since it charges from its own power brick.
  • A small self-powered appliance — a kiosk, display, music or label device from a manufacturer using that same adapter chipset, with “EKAN” as its factory hostname. The metronomic, never-sleeping DHCP pattern fits an appliance at least as well as a tablet.

We deliberately are not asserting which. The network cannot tell the difference between these two, and guessing would not serve you.

UnresolvedWhat we cannot tell you yet

  • Whether it is genuinely an iPad, or something else entirely.
  • Who owns it, who connected it, and whether it is sanctioned.
  • Exactly when it first appeared. Our visibility begins 2026-08-04 and it was already there — so it is at least a month old, possibly much older.
  • What “EKAN” stands for. It matches no product name we can find.

One thing worth your attention. This is not an active incident and nothing is under attack — but an unidentified device is sitting on the VLAN that also carries manager laptops and desk phones, on a port with no 802.1X authentication. That port would not have stopped anyone from plugging in. Whether that matters is a policy call rather than a technical fault, and it is your call to make. We are flagging it because you would want to know, not because we see anything malicious in its behaviour.

How to settle it

1
Look at the port — 30 seconds. BOH port 23 runs to the back office. Physically tracing the cable answers “what is it and whose is it” faster and more reliably than anything we can do remotely.
2
Pull Meraki’s own device profile — remote, about a minute. Meraki keeps a fuller record for this client than the dashboard view shows, including its device-type prediction, operating system, the hostname it broadcast on joining, and its true first-seen date. That would confirm or rule out “iPad” without anyone visiting the store. We can run this for you on request.
3
Or check it yourself in Meraki. Open Network-wide › Clients › EKAN. That page carries Meraki’s device-type prediction, operating system and first-seen date — often enough on its own to confirm or rule out “iPad”.
4
If it turns out to be unsanctioned, the port can be shut from the Argus console immediately — no site visit needed. We would rather identify it first than disable something a manager depends on.

What we recommend

  • Identify before acting. The device has been stable for a month; there is no urgency that justifies cutting it off blind.
  • Give it a real name once identified. Right now it shows as “EKAN” to anyone looking at the network, which is how it became a question in the first place. A named device is one nobody has to investigate twice.
  • The port draws no power, so PoE can be switched off on it if you want the config to match reality. Cosmetic, entirely optional.
  • Consider whether the staff VLAN should accept unauthenticated devices at the port level. That is a broader posture decision than this one device, and worth having deliberately rather than by default.
