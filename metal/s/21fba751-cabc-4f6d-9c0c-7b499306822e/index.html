Guest Recovery · Food-Safety Screening

Where Mendo stands on food-illness screening — the facts.

The pain

A guest writes in: “I got sick after eating at Mendo.” If that message gets an automated “so sorry — here’s a coupon, come back soon,” a coupon just turned a health complaint into a brand, legal, and public-health problem. That message can land any day, in any language, buried in thousands of others. Getting it wrong once is the mistake you can’t take back.

The facts

1
Your inbox is mostly not guests.
431,260 Zendesk tickets — only ~69,400 (16%) are real guest feedback. The rest is invoices, HR, IT, and bots (Accounts Payable alone is 36% of the total). Measured, not estimated.
2
A screen exists, and it’s built to fail safe.
No guest gets an automated reply unless two independent AI models both clear it as safe. Any flag, any error, any doubt → it stops and routes to a human, and the run ends. On a suspected illness it never auto-replies — the reply path is closed by design.
3
It is not yet proven on your history.
It runs in a sandbox today — no real guest has been contacted. Its track record so far is a 12-case test, not your years of real tickets. Until it’s run over the real history, “it works” is a design claim, not a measured one.
4
Proving it is cheap.
Running the screen over every real guest ticket in your history costs ~$27 one-time; keeping it monitored is under $3/month. The bill is a rounding error — the actual work is a person reviewing the handful it flags.
5
The off-the-shelf alternative can’t be checked.
Zendesk’s own AI cannot be replayed over past tickets — there is no way to ask “would it have caught this one?” You’d be arming it blind.

Our take

Opinion

Spend the ~$27 and prove it before you rely on it. It is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy against the one error you cannot undo — an automated coupon in reply to a sick guest.

Do not arm it in production until the proof shows zero missed illness across the real history, with a human on every flagged case. Cheap to prove, catastrophic to skip.

Straight about the limits: the screen uses AI to make the safe/not-safe call, and two AI models can share a blind spot — “fail-safe by design” describes the architecture (a closed reply path on any doubt), not a guarantee the models are perfect. That is exactly why the one-time proof, and a human on every flag, are non-negotiable.
