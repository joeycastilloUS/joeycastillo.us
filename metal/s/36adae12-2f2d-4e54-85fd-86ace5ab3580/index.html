CASE PATTERNS · NOISE CONTROLS REV 12026-07-26

Noise controls, rev 1

Tune the case-pattern matcher from the tab instead of from a terminal — mute a signal, judge a case, export what you decided

Rev 1 · live at argus.com.ai/app → Salesforce → Case Patterns · 2026-07-26

What changed: the tab used to be read-only — it showed you 933 matched cases and you had to take the signal list on faith. Now you can switch a signal off and watch the count recompute, mark individual cases right or wrong, and export both. Every control operates on the matcher's real rule, not on the display.

1. Mute a signal

Every pattern is driven by a handful of literal search terms, each carrying a weight. A case matches when its score reaches 2 — one weight-2 term on its own, or two weight-1 terms together.

Click the speaker beside any term and it switches off. The match set is then recomputed under that same rule, with the muted term removed from scoring. It is not hiding rows. It answers the only question that matters about a noisy term: what is it actually costing me?

CP-008  Device Group / Bump-to-Print          155 cases
  mute "not printing"                     ->  132
  also mute "device group"                ->   51

That is the tab's weakest pattern, measured in two clicks. Before rev 1 the only way to learn this was to edit the signal list and re-run the whole bake.

2. Judge a case

Click a case to open it in the raw drill and read the real subject and description. Then call it on-pattern or noise.

Once you have judged a few, every signal starts reporting its own hit rate from your reads — "1/2 you called on-pattern", "0/6 you called on-pattern". A term you suspect stops being a hunch and becomes a number attached to the term that caused it.

This is the same work that produced the last correction — eight cases per pattern, read by hand, which found nine generic phrases carrying more weight than they had earned and cut the match count from 1,578 to 933 without losing a single proven case. Rev 1 moves that work out of my terminal and into your hands, where it belongs, because you know a real support case when you read one and I am inferring from vocabulary.

3. Export what you decided

One button copies your mutes and verdicts out as JSON. That is the input to the next revision of the signal list — the loop closes: the tab now produces the evidence that improves the matcher, instead of me sampling and deciding alone.

4. What is deliberately limited — and labelled as such

LOCALThis browser onlyMutes and verdicts live in this browser. Nothing is shared, nothing syncs, and the pane says so rather than implying a team-wide state that does not exist. Export is how they leave.
SAMPLE200-case listing capThe bake lists the first 200 cases per pattern. For a bigger pattern — CP-007 has 428 — a mute can only recompute over those 200, so the new number is a floor, not the whole set. The row is labelled sample and the case list spells it out. A flagged floor beats a confident wrong number.
STILL CAPPEDRecall ceilingThree patterns still hit the store's search ceiling, which caps what any of this can see. The fix is written and committed but not deployed — it needs a library snapshot that is not on the build machine.

5. What to look for when you review it

Three places I would expect this to fall down, listed so you can aim at them rather than hunt:

  1. The 200-cap bites on the big patterns. CP-007 at 428 cannot be fully retuned from the UI. If that gets in your way, the cap is the first thing to raise.
  2. Verdicts do not survive a different browser or machine. Fine for one operator tuning one list; wrong the moment a second person judges cases. Rev 2 would put them behind the same store the rest of the console already uses.
  3. CP-008 is still the weakest pattern even after muting. "Device group" appears in configuration requests as readily as in bump-to-print failures, and no weighting fixes that — the term is genuinely ambiguous in this corpus. It may need splitting into two patterns, or a second corroborating term, and that is a judgement about the support domain rather than about the code.

6. What rev 2 would be

  • 1Raise or remove the 200-case listing cap so every pattern is fully tunable.
  • 2Persist verdicts server-side so they survive browsers and accumulate across people.
  • 3Feed exported judgements back into the bake automatically, so a revision is a button rather than a hand-edit.
  • 4Let a signal's weight be edited in place, not just muted — muting is binary, and the real correction last time was a demotion.

None of those are started. Rev 1 is deliberately the smallest thing that lets you form an opinion, which is the point of shipping it before building more.

7. Try it

  1. Open argus.com.ai/app, Salesforce, Case Patterns. Hard-refresh once to pick up the new build.
  2. Open CP-008. Mute not printing, then device group, and watch 155 fall to 51.
  3. Open three or four of the survivors, read them, and call each one. Then look back at the signal list — the terms now carry your hit rate.
  4. Hit Export judgements and send it back. That is what the next signal revision is built from.
