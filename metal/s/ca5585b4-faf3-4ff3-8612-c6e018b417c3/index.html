What shipped

New EnvSlots.csPure, testable: three slot names, five distinct verdicts (Ok/Missing/Unknown/NotConfigured/Forbidden), env-qualified ledger keys. No clients — nothing to mock.
Gate 1unknown → 400 always. known-but-unconfigured → 503, never conflated with 400 (ops diagnosis stays deterministic). missing → 400 only under ZENDESK_ENV_STRICT; until then legacy target + CRITICAL ENV-MISSING.
Gate 2Six sandbox-only routes + promo-claim reject prod with 403, decided before the configured check — posture never depends on deployment state.
Frozen keySlot name IS the HMAC key. Arm A stays the literal sandbox. No mapping table to misdeclare, no second source of truth.
LedgersSentDrafts + PromoLedger env-qualified — a reused id in one arm can never dedupe the other.
Arm BClient wired from ZENDESK_SANDBOX_B_*, sitting unconfigured (correctly 503-ing) until creds land.

The pairing did real work — twice

1. The navigator killed the driver's rename. Opus proposed addressing arm A as sandbox-a with a separate frozen CodeKey. Fable found the disqualifier in our own source: web/app/index.html:1894 silently falls back to "prod" on an unrecognised env id. The rename would have pointed every parked browser at production after deploy — no error, no prompt. Design reverted to name==key.

2. The navigator FAILED the driver's build. Two blockers, both invisible that day because sandbox-b was unconfigured and 503s first:

  • SentDrafts was never env-qualified — the driver did PromoLedger and stopped. The moment arm B's creds landed, a reused draft_id would return created:false from the wrong arm: the exact false-dedupe the design existed to prevent.
  • Two responses lied about the destination — alert-create and recovery-send hardcoded env:"sandbox", so an arm-B write would report itself as arm A in the audit surface this whole design exists to make truthful.

Both fixed, plus sync-object's declaration forced to match its destination and nine audit lines rebound to the real arm. Re-verified PASS — Fable re-ran the build and tests itself rather than taking the driver's word.

Tests

143/143 (from 128). Eleven new env cases pin the contract, including "Forbidden is decided before Configured", "unknown never resolves to prod", and the 400-vs-503 separation — on top of the Phase-0 golden locks that freeze arm A's code and prove cross-arm divergence.
