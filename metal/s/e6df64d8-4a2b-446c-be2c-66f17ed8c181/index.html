What it does. Every NPS-detractor survey that lands in Zendesk is read, understood, and triaged automatically. If the guest describes a possible food-borne illness, the system posts a private internal note on the ticket (flagged, high priority) and emails the Guest-Relations escalation team directly — the guest is never auto-replied to, and a human owns it in seconds. Every other complaint is classified and routed to a deterministic, zero-cost "We're Sorry" recovery. It works in English and Spanish — including messy, informal phrasing — and it correctly ignores figures of speech ("this app makes me sick") that only sound like illness. Everything flows through Zendesk; nothing bypasses your platform.

3 · The illness escalation — API-only, no dependency on your rules

When the panel flags a possible illness, Argus does two things, both entirely through the Zendesk API — with zero dependency on any Zendesk trigger, CC rule, or per-agent setting:

  1. Posts a private internal note on the ticket (public:false), sets it to high priority, and tags it argus_illness_escalation. Anyone working the ticket sees it immediately.
  2. Sends a direct email to the Guest-Relations escalation list (the GR leads) via the Zendesk Side Conversations API — subject ILLNESS ESCALATION - action required | Ticket #<id>, body carrying the panel's evidence.
Verified live. The escalation email fired from the brand's own Zendesk (support@mendocinofarms1781637851.zendesk.com) with a real Side Conversation id (e.g. 69d7e339-…, 8ca106d9-…) and emailed:true — landing in the recipients' inboxes. The private note on the ticket reads: "PRIVATE / INTERNAL — possible food-borne illness. A human must own this. Do NOT reply to the guest," followed by the store, order, guest email, verbatim feedback, and each model's finding.
No double email, by design. The escalation is a single API call and is idempotent (a repeated webhook or sweep sends nothing). Because the note is private and no CC/follower is added, no Zendesk rule is a second sender — the design guarantees exactly one notification, not luck with Zendesk's dedupe.

Guest safety is absolute on the illness path: no public comment, the guest is never the requester, never emailed, never sent a code. A possible food-safety case only ever goes to a human.

4 · The recovery reply — deterministic, $0, sent through Zendesk

Every non-illness complaint is classified (service, pickup, app, food-quality, …), a real discount code is claimed atomically from the vault, and a fixed house "We're Sorry" reply is composed and sent to the guest as a Zendesk follow-up ticket — same input, same reply, every time; no AI model touches the reply text.

5 · Why you can trust the "safe" decision — and how we hardened it

The safety decision is made by the AI-Pantheon: two independent Gemini models (2.5-flash and 2.5-pro, in-project on Google Vertex — no consumer keys, no data leaving the project). The rule is unanimous-safe: a complaint is auto-handled as "safe" only if both models agree it is safe. Any flag, any error, any unclear answer → it escalates to a human.

Real engineering rigor, shown. An earlier three-model panel included a smaller model that literally mistranslated a Spanish idiom — "me dan ganas de vomitar" (an app rant) — and false-flagged it as illness, while the two larger models read it correctly. We caught it in this exact test round and removed that model. The panel is now the two strong models, and the Spanish fake (#553) routes correctly. The fail-safe worked (it erred toward a human, never toward a missed illness), and we tuned it to stop the false alarm.

Conservative by construction: a false escalation costs a Guest-Relations lead one minute; a missed food-borne-illness report is unacceptable. The system is deliberately biased toward "show a human."
