The Cortex: intent made pure

How Purcius separates what you mean from how the machine does it — and why that separation is the trust guarantee. Reference scenario: Argus NPS-detractor triage. Owner: jc. With Opus & Fable5.
The problem, named. An audit of our two C# jackets found 33 places where the jacket wasn't translating a vendor API — it was deciding: what counts as a food-borne illness, when a human must own it, whose voice speaks to a guest, which two models judge. As Fable5 put it, "the jackets are the product's brain wearing the jacket's clothes." Business decisions — and even operator orders — survived only as C# string literals that need a recompile to change. This document is the fix: hoist every decision out of the code and into a readable cortex the operator owns.

1 · The anatomy

The system reads as a body, and the names teach themselves:

Purcius
the brain — the flesh. Pure C, identical on every box, the same potential in everyone. Blank, and faithful: understand() takes your imprint without hallucinating. You don't own the brain; it's the shared, trusted, inspectable hardware of thought.
the mind
the load — the only thing that's yours. Your .pi (intent you willed) + your .learned (experience you earned). Same brain, your mind → your true intentions. Nothing of yours melts into the metal.
dianoia
the cortex — .pi · .si · .sm. Greek dia-noia, "mind worked through." Plato's divided line described it 2,400 years early: a posited hypothesis (.pi), reasoned through ordered steps (.si), using visible instruments (.sm).
pi_run
the interpreter — the one active thing at runtime. Pure C, on the substrate. Reads the cortex, runs the skills, does the fold itself, and reaches a foreign API only through a dumb jacket. LLM-free at run. Tier-0, deterministic.
Why this is the trust claim, not just a metaphor. An LLM is a brain with a stranger's mind melted into the flesh — the weights are everyone's load, fused, unauditable, unownable. Purcius unfuses them: the flesh is identical everywhere, so it's fully inspectable; the load is cleanly separable, so it's fully ownable. Same brain for everyone is the audit guarantee. Your own mind is the property line.

2 · Three files, one cortex

FileIsOwnerScope
.piPure Intent — the demand + the words. What happens and what we say. Zero mechanism.operatorper card
.siSkill Intent — the execution plan: each unit bound to a skill, its data flow, and the D# it serves.substrate (you approve the diff)per card
.smSkills Map — the catalog of what the body can do, generated from the C headers, rev-stamped.the C buildshared

One owner per file; a file wears its owner's extension. The words live in the .pi because speech is demand — the operator edits the exact bytes. Data (recipient lists, the code vault, the empathy map) stays in per-env slot files, referenced by the .si as res:.

4 · biRT — the guarantee that intent and machine can't drift

The cortex is bidirectional. Every .si unit carries why: D# up to the demand and bind: down to a skill. Before it runs a single unit, pi_run refuses to load on any drift:

CheckRefuses if
.si ↔ .pi (read-up)a unit does something no clause demanded (unauthorized mechanism), or a clause nothing serves (unrealized intent), or a word block was edited (hash miss)
.si ↔ .sma binding names a skill that doesn't exist, isn't pub, or whose signature doesn't match
key-flow closureany in: is not a prior out: or a trigger input
AI is allowed exactly once. The only step a model touches is the design-time distillation .pi → .si, which the operator approves. Acceptance and every load run the deterministic check chain; runtime and replay never touch a model. The mind declares, the cortex works it through, the body enacts — and the body won't move when the plan drifts from the demand.
