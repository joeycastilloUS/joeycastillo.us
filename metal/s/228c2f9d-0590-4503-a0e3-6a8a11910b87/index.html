Three NPS cards

One card per band, so each says only what is true of its band. The runtime cannot route to that today — three small C changes unlock it.

Paired. Design only; no code or card touched.

The blocker

pi_run.c:455       the target card is ONE static name from the .si.
               No unit can write it — there is no micro-op that could.

pi_run.c:460-461   a card that was dispatched to CANNOT dispatch onward.
               "nested fan-out refused"

Every card-only wiring dies on one of those:

WiringDies because
Sweep bands, dispatches to one of threeThe score does not exist yet — it appears only after the card reads the ticket.
Entry card bands, dispatches onwardThe recursion guard refuses it.
Three cards, each skips if not its bandThe skip cannot be written. A card's only branch is the exit gate, and no micro-op can compare a score to a range to feed it. biRT refuses any bind not in the catalog.

So three cards costs runtime work, not just card files. You have already accepted a substrate change for the generative replies; this adds to it.

The shape

mendo-nps-sweep          finds alerts, dispatches each ticket          unchanged
 └─ mendo-nps-triage     ENTRY. read · dedup claim · illness panel ·
                         escalate-or-band.  Owns the safety floor.
     ├─ mendo-nps-promoter    9-10   thank you  + $10   model-written
     ├─ mendo-nps-passive     7-8    what can we do + $20   model-written
     └─ mendo-nps-detractor   0-6    "We're Sorry"          deterministic

Four cards, not three. The dedup claim and the illness panel must exist exactly once. The panel in particular has to sit above the split — a guest can score 9 and still report a symptom — so it cannot live inside any reply card.

Folding the entry role into one of the three would work, but that card becomes privileged: promoter tickets would flow through a card named "detractor," and the safety floor would live somewhere arbitrary. A named entry card is the cleaner reading of the same four files.

What each card holds

triage — entryread · dedup claim · understand · illness panel · escalate · band decisionD1–D8, D13, D15 — the whole safety floor

No reply card carries the dedup gate — see the failure modes below. Each re-reads the alert because a dispatched card starts with a fresh ledger and inherits nothing but the ticket id.

D14 rides on all four. "Sandbox until jc arms prod" is env-scoped, not path-scoped — it is carried by every card's env: header, and arming is per card. That matters here: four cards means four things to arm, and a card left unarmed composes silently instead of sending. Nothing else is unassigned; D1–D15 are fully partitioned.

The prize: D9 and D10 stop being conditional.

Today they would have to read "on the detractor path, no model writes a word a guest reads…" — a guarantee with an exception clause. Split, they stay exactly as written and become the plain law of one card. Each card can be read alone and believed.

The amendment burden moves rather than vanishes: D1 still changes — "Every NPS-detractor alert" becomes "Every NPS alert" on the entry card, and the detractor-scoped naming carries through the sweep with it.

A second hop breaks three things a depth counter alone does not fix:

  • The resolver snapshot is one shared buffer — nested, the inner dispatch overwrites the outer's, and the sweep restores the wrong map. Must be per-depth.
  • The per-ticket child store path is derived from environment and ticket id, so entry and band card compute the same file for the same ticket. Needs a discriminator.
  • Each hop gets a fresh ledger — only the ticket id is passed. Hence the re-read in every reply card.

Checked and safe under nesting: the card-directory capture, id pre-tokenisation, the fan-out bound, the environment constant. The depth counter must decrement on every error return.

Failure modes to design against

  • A reply card that keeps the dedup gate fails silently and reads green. It finds the tag the entry card claimed, exits cleanly, returns success — so the entry card counts it dispatched. Every guest triaged, nobody replied to, every counter healthy. Dedup is entry-only.
  • "Dispatched" stops meaning "replied." A clean exit counts as success, so a whole band can go unanswered behind a green number. Needs a per-band reply counter.
  • Escalation must stay in the entry card. Duplicated into reply cards, a guest could be escalated twice — D8 says once, ever.
  • A missing catalog row is the good failure. biRT refuses the card at load, loudly, rather than running it half-bound.

The standing cost

Cards reach the primes by manual copy, unverified, and that path drifted silently within the last two days. One card is one thing to keep in sync across two seats; four is four. This does not change the decision — it prices it. And it is reversible: the demands are written identically either way, so collapsing back to one card would be a file move, not a redesign.

Unknowns

  • Whether each dispatched card needs its own addressing row on the primes, or inherits the entry card's. Node-local; not knowable from these repos.
  • Whether one generative micro-op serves both new cards or needs per-band variants.
  • Whether the live vault claim honours denominations — the local store does; the claim that runs is bridge-side.
  • Whether promoter and passive alerts reach Zendesk today at all. If only detractors arrive, this is correct and inert until the form sends the rest.

When to revisit

When the card deploy becomes a script — that retires the only real argument against the split. And if a fifth band or a second customer appears, because four cards becomes eight and the sync cost stops being theoretical.
