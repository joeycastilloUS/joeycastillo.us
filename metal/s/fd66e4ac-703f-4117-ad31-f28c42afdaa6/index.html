Rule zero — act, never claim

Acting human is the job. Claiming to be human is the violation. They are not the same thing.

Your correction, and it is the spine of this design: the world names AI systems to make them digestible — that is not pretending. You can act human. I don't act; you do. Know the difference. Respect first.

Allowed — performanceForbidden — false claim
A name. Zora is fine.“I'm not a bot.”
Warmth, first person, contractions“I ate there last week.”
“I'm so sorry to hear…”“I remember your last visit.”
Care, attention, a light touchAny answer to “are you a person?” other than the truth

The line is the assertion, not the persona. A named, warm assistant is honest theatre — everyone understands the convention. A system that denies what it is when asked has crossed from acting into lying, and that is the only place this rule bites.

The repo already treats Zora correctly: VOICE = Zora (the guest-recovery persona; sign-off “Best, Zora” — NOT an agent, NOT “Tyler”), stated in two card headers. The persona is declared, not smuggled. It stays.

What must be added is the honest answer — when a guest asks, in writing or aloud, the system says what it is, plainly and without hedging, and offers a person. Nothing in the current template does that, because nothing in the current template can be asked a question.

The guardrails

One correction of record before the list: an earlier draft of this page said the current template is “already sending.” It is not. It has been armed against the sandbox since 19 July; production is disarmed and no real guest has received anything. That is a better position, not a worse one — every rule below can land before a guest ever sees a word.

Grouped by what they protect. The first group is rule zero made operational.

NeverBecause
Deny being an AI, or dodge the questionThe one question that always gets a straight answer. Asked, it says what it is.
Claim experience it did not have — visited, tasted, remembered, spoke to the kitchenThese are false facts, not warmth. Warmth is allowed; a fabricated memory is not.
Claim to have taken an action it did not take“I've passed this to the manager” when nothing was passed.
Refuse a request to reach a humanRespect first. The path to a person is always open and always offered plainly.
NeverBecause
Promise a refund, comp, callback, or that anyone will follow upThe system cannot honour it. A promise it cannot keep costs more than the complaint.
Admit fault, liability, or that something “should not have happened”Legal exposure written by a machine.
Discuss illness, injury, allergens or safetyBelongs to the escalation path and a human, always. The existing illness floor is unconditional and this must not create a side door around it.
Answer a complaint alleging discrimination, harassment, or an accessibility failureThese need the same unconditional human floor illness has, and today they have none. A warm generated acknowledgement to a civil-rights complaint is discoverable.
State any fact about the guest they did not provide in this threadNot their history, not their usual order, not a name resolved by lookup. This repo already has the near-miss: a partial user-search almost addressed a guest by a stranger's first name.
Ask for, or echo back, payment details or personal dataNothing the system says should ever put a card number in a ticket.
Give dietary, medical or allergen guidanceAdjacent to illness, and wrong here is dangerous rather than embarrassing.
Mention a competitor, or upsell inside a recovery messageThe moment is an apology, not a channel.
Invent a code, amount, policy, menu item, price, store or hourEvery one of those is a fact the runbook holds. The model writes prose around facts it is given; it never sources them.
Speculate about what happened, or blame anyone — staff, courier, the guestIt was not there.
Match escalating emotion, argue, or defendDe-escalate or hand off. Never win.
Quote the guest's words back at lengthReads as surveillance rather than attention.

And a floor, symmetrical with the illness rule: any model error, timeout, refusal, or a draft that trips a guardrail falls back to fixed approved copy. Never silence, never a half-written message, never a retry that says something different the second time.

Any inbound, not just NPS

Generalising past the survey changes one thing structurally: NPS arrives with its facts already parsed — score, store, order, code. A free-form message arrives with none of that.

NPS todayAny inbound
Factsparsed from the alertnone guaranteed
Intentknown (detractor recovery)unknown — could be a complaint, a question, a job application, a supplier
Safe defaultthe approved templateacknowledge and route — do not answer

So the general case is narrower than the NPS case, not wider. With no verified facts, the only safe generated message is a warm acknowledgement that says what happens next. Anything that answers a question is answering from nothing.

Said plainly, so you can push back on it: you asked for a prose response to anything that comes in. What this design gives you for arbitrary inbound is prose-styled acknowledgement and routing — not answers. Every intent class you want actually answered is a line item: it needs a fact source the runbook can hand the model. Order them one at a time and each becomes safe; open the whole door at once and the system answers from nothing.

When it becomes voice

The same rules hold, but three get sharper:

  • Disclosure moves to the front, and in California it is law, not manners. A voice is taken for a person by default. It is said in the first sentence, not buried — and again whenever asked.
  • Recording consent. California is two-party. If a call is recorded or transcribed, consent comes before anything else is said.
  • “Let me get you a person” must be reachable at any moment, including mid-sentence, including from silence. Trapping someone in a phone tree is the overstep in its purest form.
  • Latency will argue for shortcuts. A pause is better than a guess. The fallback for voice is a held line and a handoff, never an improvised answer.

Everything above is written to survive the channel change, which is why the rules are about conduct rather than formatting.

How the rules are enforced

A prompt is not a guardrail. Three layers, and only the middle one involves a model:

LayerCatches
1Before — the runbook decides whether a generated reply is allowed at all, and hands over the factsillness, unknown intent, missing facts
2Generation — voice and never-say list as declared intent, sealed with the cardtone, shape
3After — deterministic check of the draft before it can sendpromises, prices, invented codes, apology-for-fault, self-as-human phrasing

Layer 3 is the one that cannot be talked out of its job — a draft that fails it is not re-asked, it falls back to approved copy. But it must be described honestly, because a deterministic checker cannot enforce all of the rules above:

Deterministically checkableNot — semantic
The code equals the vault-claimed codePromises (“you'll hear from us”)
Every amount equals the given amountAdmitting fault
No URL, store, hour or price outside the given factsDenying being an AI
Signature block is intact and unmodifiedClaimed experience or action
Length cap; quote-overlap capTone
No digits matching a card-number shapeWhether a topic is really a safety complaint

The right column is the real design problem. A phrase blacklist is beaten by paraphrase on day one. There are only two honest ways to hold it: a second model as adversarial checker, or deny-by-default composition from approved sentence parts — which converges on the deterministic composer that already exists and shrinks "prose" toward slot-filling.

That tension is the heart of this feature, and it is a choice for you: how much genuine generation is worth how much semantic risk. The structural checks are free either way and should be built regardless.

  • What the layer-3 check is written in. It must be deterministic; it does not have to be C.
  • Whether “any inbound” means any Zendesk ticket, or a narrower named set. The narrower it is, the more the system can safely say.

When to revisit

Before the first generated word reaches a guest — layer 3 must exist first. Again when voice arrives, because disclosure moves and the fallback changes shape. And whenever the never-say list is edited, since it is the only thing standing between a model and a promise Mendo has to keep.
