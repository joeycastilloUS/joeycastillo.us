Horizontal tabs — you're right, and it's not tomorrow

You like tabs across the top better than the rail. Both passes agree that is a legitimate preference — and both independently classify doing it globally tomorrow as scope creep. The evidence:

index.html:15   #tabnav{position:fixed;left:0;width:188px}
index.html:16   .tabnav-inner{flex-direction:column}
index.html:94   .wrap{margin-left:188px}
index.html:339  #brand-strip — reserves the same 188px
index.html:36   @media(max-width:640px) — re-derives all of it

Five interlocking rule sets, plus the app-switcher lives inside the rail and is built for a 188px column. And the Zendesk app will have 12 tabs, several with live count badges — they do not fit one row at content width, so you would be inventing overflow UX at midnight, across all six apps. It changes every screen you are about to demo, for zero functional gain tomorrow. 5 points and a full visual regression pass.

What we can do tonight for ~1 point: render the Playbooks pane's own sub-navigation as horizontal tabs. You get to react to the top-tab feel live, on stage, in the new surface — and nothing else in the console moves. Then decide the global conversion with real reaction behind it, behind a localStorage flag so it is reversible.

Riskiest item

1.4 — making the rulebook legible without faking it. The .pi is prose-heavy policy. A rushed summarizer that misrenders your own rules is worse than showing nothing, and fake UI has already been rejected twice here. De-risk: the verbatim source is always on screen and is the authority; anything friendlier sits beside it, never instead of it; playbooks whose dials are not mapped yet say “view only — not yet mapped” in plain words rather than rendering an empty editor.
