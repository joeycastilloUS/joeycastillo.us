4 · The filter — and the survivor count G

KEEP ticket IFF:
  group ∈ {"Guest Relations", "GR Escalation Group"}     -- guest-facing queue
  AND subject NOT LIKE 'Automatic reply:%'               -- kill OOO / marketing bounces
  AND subject NOT LIKE 'Follow%up from Mendo%'           -- kill outbound marketing
  AND subject NOT LIKE 'Guest Relations: Press Inquiry%' -- kill press
  AND requester NOT IN {known bulk/system bots}          -- belt-and-suspenders
  AND has ≥1 guest-authored PUBLIC comment               -- the guest's own words

G ≈ 69,400 genuine guest-feedback records — ~16% of 431,260 (conservative range 66k–70k). Operator’s hypothesis confirmed, hard: roughly 1 ticket in 6 is a guest telling you about their experience.

One layer is still an estimate: “has a guest-authored public comment” was spot-checked via /replay, not counted across all 431,260. Nailing it exactly is one pass over the raw comment public-flags — Phase 0 below does it.

7 · The proposals — three tiers + recurring at G≈69,400, batch, lean

LOW — sieve funnel ~$2–4 one-time

$0 lexicon sieve over all G → candidates (~2–4%) + 2K control; 2-Gemini batch on those ~4K only; Haiku presides flags (pennies). Get: a flagged-case inventory + rough precision. Give up: the recall claim — sieve recall is unmeasurable, and it misses exactly the idiomatic/misspelled cases the panel exists to catch. Good as label-bootstrapping (it is Phase 1’s seed); not a defensible backtest.

MED — full-G, production-identical panel ~$27 one-time ★ recommended

The exact prod screen (2.5-flash + 2.5-pro, verbatim, unanimous-safe) over all G via Vertex batch (~$26), through the staged rollout. Haiku 4.5 presides only on panel disagreements (flash≠pro, ~1–3%) + a 2% random audit (~$1). The $0 sieve runs in parallel as the missed-illness cross-check. Get: the number that describes the screen you actually ship — flag rate, precision on adjudicated flags, recall vs the constructed label set, per-language breakdown (the #532 Spanish class gets its own row). Give up: nothing money buys back.

HIGH — every record, 3-Gemini + heavy presiding ~$140 (Opus) / ~$255 (Fable)

All of G, 3-model Gemini panel + Opus/Fable presiding every record. Panel ~$28; Opus-all ~$113 (Fable-all ~$226). Get: a frontier-model second opinion on ~67K records it will rubber-stamp, plus better adjudication on the ~2K interesting ones — and the sentence “a frontier model reviewed every guest ticket.” Give up: ~$100–230 for information you’d get by pointing Opus at only the ~2K disagreements+flags (which MED already does for +$3). A rhetorical purchase, not a statistical one.

Your explicit ask — “cost all three Gemini, even the one we threw out.” Done: the third seat (Flash-Lite, batch) adds only ~$1.3 over all G. Including it means the backtest measures a screen you don’t ship. So it’s priced and available as a “what-if we’d kept it” comparison lane — but the prod-valid number is 2-Gemini. Your call which one headlines; neither is hidden.

8 · Reliability — where each tier lies to you

  • No ground-truth labels exist. Every “recall” is against a label set you construct (sieve ∪ panel-flags ∪ audit-sample, human-adjudicated) — biased toward findable cases. The doc says “recall 1.00 on N adjudicated illness cases,” never bare “100% recall.” Unknown-unknowns are bounded only by the audit sample’s binomial CI.
  • LOW can’t measure recall → unfalsifiable headline. Keep it out of a CEO doc.
  • MED is honest but conditional on: Phase-0 reject audit clean; batch prompt = bridge prompt byte-identical; verbatim extraction (not thread dumps). Unanimous-safe biases errors toward escalation (a false flag costs a human minute; a miss is unforgivable) — the survivable direction.
  • HIGH with the 3rd Gemini measures a non-prod screen — the most expensive tier yields the least transferable number.

Minimum bar to arm prod: Phase-0 reject audit clean; recall = 1.00 on the full adjudicated label set; every sieve-positive/panel-safe record human-cleared; flag rate stable Phases 2→4; false-flag rate low enough that ops review load is sustainable; the frozen 1K bench committed as the permanent regression gate.

9 · Recommendation + first move

MED — ~$27 one-time, <$3/month ongoing.

It’s the only tier that backtests the screen you actually ship. LOW saves ~$25 by forfeiting the one number the exercise exists to produce; HIGH spends ~$100–230 to rubber-stamp ~67K clean tickets. Spend the saved money on adjudication time, not on a frontier model stamping the obvious.

First move (all $0, no panel calls): (1) run guest_filter.c over the raw → nail G and the DQ histogram exactly, including the comment-flag layer; (2) re-run the 12-row pirun_bench once for the measured $/verdict; (3) Phase 1 — 100 seed records through the real bridge to pin reference verdicts. Three cheap steps turn every estimate here into a fact before a dollar of scale is spent.
