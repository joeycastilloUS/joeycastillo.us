Weekly Velocity — People-Hours per Site

The labor it cost to finish each site, rolled up by install week. 1 person on site for 1 hour = 1 point. Because the install weeks each ran a different number of nights (and therefore a different number of sites), the fair week-over-week comparison is people-hours PER SITE — it normalizes out the short weeks. Weekly totals and night/site counts are in the supporting table. Now covering 5 install weeks (Aug 2–); Week 5 is in progress (Nights 15–18 so far).

Velocity — People-Hours per Site, by Week

People-hours per site, per week — lower = leaner. This is the headline metric because it is comparable across weeks of different length: a short week naturally logs fewer total hours simply because it touched fewer sites, so only the per-site figure isolates how lean the crew actually got. Labor per site fell from 25.0/site in Week 1 to 10.9/site in Week 5 so far — a ~56% reduction — as the crew matured, standardized, and stopped paying the early-rollout tax. Note Week 5 is in progress (4 nights so far); its figures may still grow if the week’s calendar has a remaining install night. Per-site is the honest yardstick.

  • Week 1 cost 25.0 people-hours per site — the crew was still learning the install, the Konductor print outage hit Night 4, and the early nights ran wire-to-wire long.
  • Week 5 is running at 10.9 people-hours per site — a ~56% reduction from Week 1 and the leanest week per site of the rollout. Caveat: Week 5 is in progress (4 nights so far, Sunday–Wednesday); its figures may still grow if the week’s calendar has a remaining install night. Night 18 alone was the leanest single night of the rollout (37.6 people-hours, four 2-person crews).
  • Weeks 3 and 4 converged (13.6 vs 13.7 per site) at a steady, repeatable floor — and Week 5’s four nights show the crew still getting leaner, dropping under 11/site.
  • Comparing weekly totals would be misleading (a short week logs a small total just by touching fewer sites). Per-site is the only figure that lets you compare a 1- or 2-night week to a 4-night week fairly.

Honesty Note — Why Per-Site, Not Total (and Week 5 Is Partial)

Week 5 is IN PROGRESS — 4 nights (Sunday–Wednesday, Nights 15–18) have run so far.

  • Week 5’s total of 174.6 people-hours and per-site of 10.9 cover those four nights / 16 sites — already the leanest week of the rollout. The figures may still grow if the week’s calendar has a remaining install night; treat 10.9/site as a near-settled read, not necessarily final.
  • Same discipline as the Week 2 caveat: Week 2 was a short week — only 2 nights and 8 sites. Its total of 133.1 people-hours is small because it touched half as many sites, not because the crew was suddenly twice as lean.
  • On the fair, normalized metric — people-hours per site — Week 2 lands at 16.6/site, cleanly between Week 1 (25.0) and Week 3 (13.6). That is the honest reading: better than the early tax, not yet at the mature floor.
  • This is exactly why the headline chart plots per-site and the totals are shown only as a reference figure. Comparing raw weekly totals across weeks of different length would reward short weeks and penalize full ones — the opposite of measuring leanness.
