Install Night 1 — what happened

New kitchen screens installed overnight 2026-08-02, live for open 2026-08-03.
180 Broadway · Culver City · Porter Ranch · Sacramento (Midtown). Crews from C1 Techs; oversight by Mendocino Farms.

All four stores opened on the new system. All four also had something go wrong — ten issues, five still open. Two of them aren’t store problems, they’re instruction problems: the same fault will happen at the next stores unless the instructions are fixed first.

Plain words: Playbook = the picture-by-picture instructions for one store. KDS = the kitchen screen showing orders. Ingest = the small box that feeds orders to those screens. Expo = the station where finished orders are checked before they go out. POS = the till/register system. Checkout = crew submits photos and a form at the end of the night; that’s what gets reviewed.

The three that need a decision

1. A screen was put on the wrong side — and nobody told us OPEN EXC-008 · Porter Ranch

The instructions say the Expo screen goes on the left. It was installed on the right. The crew didn’t call to ask.

Why it matters: not the screen — the silence. Everything else that differed last night was raised and approved. This one was only caught because one person read the checkout photos at 05:19. That review can’t catch four stores a night for six weeks.

Where it stands: crew is due back at 07:45 to move it. Not moved yet — that time hasn’t come round at time of writing.

Evidence Checkout photos show the screen on the right; the store’s playbook text reads “left”. Store identity confirmed in the same message thread.

2. Crews couldn’t log in to test — the step isn’t in the instructions FIX BEFORE TONIGHT EXC-003 · Broadway + Culver City

To test the screens, the crew has to log into the till system. The login code they were given didn’t work. Two stores stalled until roughly 01:30, when a temporary elevated account was created to unblock them.

Why it matters: this is a missing page in the instruction template, not a store fault — so every remaining store hits it. Only 16 of 94 store playbooks have been written. Fixing the template now costs one edit; fixing it later costs 94.

Evidence — Elbert Nghiem, confirming the gap “We didn’t include in our playbooks. We should.”

3. The order box won’t stay online WITH VENDOR EXC-009

The Ingest box shows as offline, and stayed that way at more than one store. Everything fixable on site was tried: restart, full power-off, and a different socket on the network switch.

Why it matters: this was the single biggest time cost of the night, and it happened while a store was still being installed. It is now with Konductor (the supplier). We have no way to escalate other than asking — that needs fixing before it eats a second night.

Evidence — ruled out, so the supplier can’t bounce it back The store network shows the box plugged in and on the same network as the kitchen screens that are working fine. Tested from a laptop inside the store, the box gives no reply at all — before and after restarts, on more than one socket. That points at the box itself rather than the store’s network or firewall.

What went right

  • Three of the four differences were raised and approved properly — the process works when it’s used.
  • Sacramento re-shot bad photos before anyone judged them. The first angle was misleading and nearly caused a wrong call. That’s exactly why the photo rule exists.
  • The Ingest fault went to the supplier with proof, not opinion — ruled out the network first, so it can’t be handed back.
  • Every issue was recorded with its source. Eleven pieces of evidence retained.

The honest read

Two fixes are cheap today and expensive next week — both are one edit to the instruction template while most stores are still unwritten.

And the safety net is one person. The worst miss of the night was caught by a human reading photos at 5am. At four stores a night for six weeks, that will eventually miss one.
