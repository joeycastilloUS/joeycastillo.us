Install Night 1 — After-Action Report

Konductor kitchen rollout · Mendocino Farms · installs 2026-08-02, go-live 2026-08-03
Sacramento (Midtown) · 180 Broadway · Culver City · Porter Ranch

Bottom line. All four stores were installed and went to go-live. All four also carried exceptions — ten in total, three of them blocking, five still open at time of writing. Two problems were systemic rather than site-specific: a missing playbook step that stopped testing at two stores, and an Ingest device that will not stay online at any store and is now with the vendor. Nothing was lost, but night one consumed far more of the operator’s time than the schedule assumes, and the same failures will repeat on 8/3 unless the playbook fixes land first.

2. Systemic findings — these are not store problems

EXC-003  BLOCKER  POS test code missing from the playbooks

The POS test code did not work at Broadway and Culver City. Crews could not log in to test until the operator intervened at approximately 01:30 and provisioned a temporary elevated Toast account.

Root cause, confirmed by Elbert Nghiem:

“We didn’t include in our playbooks. We should.”

This is a template defect. Every remaining install hits it until the playbook is corrected. Only 16 of 94 playbook decks have been written — correcting the template now costs almost nothing; correcting it later means 94 retrofits.

EXC-009  BLOCKER · UNRESOLVED  Ingest device will not stay online

The Ingest device will not come online consistently at any store. Every field remediation was attempted and failed:

  • Reboot · full power-down and unplug from switch · different switch ports (which rules out a single faulty port)
  • No response to ping, and no response on the API port, tested from laptops on the store LAN

Confirmed not a firewall or network issue. Meraki shows the device connected on the same VLAN as the working kitchen tablets, so this traffic never traverses the firewall. The device answers nothing above link level.

Now with Konductor (vendor). This was the single largest time cost of the night, and it ran concurrently with Sacramento still being installed.

5. What must change before the next install night

  1. Add the POS login step to the playbook template (REQ-005). This stopped testing at two stores on night one and will repeat.
  2. Correct the playbook: pole mount → desktop mount (REQ-006). The deck is out of date; crews are resolving the contradiction on site at 5am.
  3. Resolve the Ingest fault with the vendor (EXC-009) before it consumes a second night.
  4. Close the loop on EXC-008 — confirm the KDS was actually relocated.

Points 1 and 2 are both template defects. With 78 of 94 decks still unwritten, fixing them now propagates for free. Every day of delay converts a free fix into four more retrofits.

6. What worked

  • The deviation-approval loop. Three of four deviations (EXC-001, EXC-004, EXC-006) were raised by the field, reasoned through, and approved with the reason recorded — exactly what DEC-004 asks for.
  • Re-shooting bad photos before judging. At Sacramento the first images were a poor angle; the crew re-shot backed up for perspective before any decision was made. Bad framing nearly produced a wrong call — which is precisely why DEC-002 exists.
  • Structured field diagnosis. A documented test procedure turned “the dashboard says offline” into “no ICMP and no TCP on the API port from the store LAN, across reboots, power cycles and multiple switch ports” — an evidence-backed handoff to the vendor rather than an argument.
  • Every exception was captured with its source and evidence. Eleven evidence items were retained.

7. The honest read

Four for four. Every store on night one produced at least one exception. That is a signal about the process, not about four unlucky stores — and it is the most useful thing this report contains.

The one that matters most is EXC-008. Not because relocating a screen is hard, but because the deviation was silent. The other deviations were raised, discussed and approved. This one was found only because the operator personally reviewed the checkout at 05:19. That safety net does not scale: the schedule calls for roughly four installs a night for six weeks, and one person cannot be the last line of defence for all of them.

Coverage predicts exceptions. The only store with no pre-install prep notes — 180 Broadway — produced the first exception of the night. Silence in the record looked like coverage; it was not.

10. What could be better — carried to continuous improvement

  • Deviations must be called in before they are built. EXC-008 was found at checkout, not raised by the crew. The approval loop works when used — it has to be used every time.
  • Playbook corrections must land before decks are written. Two template defects (REQ-005, REQ-006) surfaced on night one, with 78 of 94 decks unwritten. That window closes a little every day.
  • Every store needs prep notes. The one store without them produced the first exception of the night.
  • Vendor escalation cannot depend on goodwill at 5am. ACT-002 — a named contact or diagnostic visibility is needed before the next wave.
  • Exception detection should not rest on one person reviewing checkouts. Roughly four installs a night for six weeks is beyond what manual review sustains.
